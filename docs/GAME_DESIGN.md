# Side Quest — game and content brief

For the intended humor, specificity, and card-writing personality, also use [Card voice and theme reference](CARD_VOICE_REFERENCE.md). It distills the reusable creative direction from Spencer's earlier CABIIN card set without importing that game's board, team, zone, or movement rules.

## Pillars

A shared deck, a lively table, one clear instruction at a time. A reveal should be delightful and a rule should make sense on one reading. No accounts, scorekeeping, or player setup. The app deals cards; the people handle the activity. The one exception to "the app only deals" is Secrets & fuses (owner decision, 2026-10-06): a card may hold a secret one player reads by holding a plaque, or a timer (a hidden fuse or a visible countdown) that keeps the card on the table until time is up. Neither is saved or tracked between cards.

## Core editorial direction

Classic party: short drinking prompts, broad group prompts, categories, playful verbal challenges, temporary rules, and dice gambles. Instructions use everyday language. Pours are printed on the card or resolved from a committed dice roll; the app never enforces a pour, and any drink can be nonalcoholic. The House deck ships exactly as supplied: blunt, specific, and without consent boilerplate.

Choose anyone to draw first. Pass the device clockwise unless a card names the next drawer. The drawer starts a category or challenge. Complete its activity before dismissing the card. Category rounds end on the first repeat or blank; no stopwatch or debate about how long someone hesitated. Every card makes its actor explicit: the drawing player, the whole table, or a named subset. A lasting rule runs either for the rest of the game or until the drawing player's next turn — never until the next card — with explicit consequences in the card text. No rule requires app bookkeeping.

## Current content

Six opt-in packs. `core` (275 cards, `src/content/core-deck.ts`) is one plain deck of three sources that play alike (merged 2026-10-24, owner: "basically the same thing and don't change gameplay"): the generated main deck (96: the sample set (25), classics and King's Cup ranks (24), and the standard expansion (47), rewritten on 2026-10-07 to be cruder and to replace bare roll-and-pour cards with table moments), the supplied Sheet1 restored verbatim in `src/content/custom.ts` (94, `house.sheet-*` IDs) and Cabin weekend (85, `cabin.*` IDs, below). The old `house` and `cabin` packs no longer exist; saved selections and games that name them read as `core`. `VIP night` (16 cards) covers birthdays and celebrations. `pokemon` (120 cards, `src/content/pokemon.ts`) translates the supplied Kanto/Johto/Hoenn board sheets into the shared-deck format and has its own mode, Pokémon League, beside Standard/Infinite. Since 2026-10-16 (owner: "the dungeon crawl is drastically more exciting") the League is a journey on the Pub Crawl engine and plays the Pokémon pack alone; the 120 deck cards play only in the plain modes. The team shares 17 HP (Pub Crawl's hearts, shown as Poké Balls). A run opens on a starter (1 of 35, from every generation and at least one of every type), whose trade-off holds for the run: Charmander's wild fights hurt one less but bosses one more, Squirtle starts with a Protect but a bag of two, Pikachu has two more HP and an X Attack and refuses to evolve. Five of the eight routes carry an evolution card (a Rare Candy, an evolution stone, "What? It's evolving!"): evolve for a drink and the starter's next form replaces its effect with a stronger one (Charmeleon, then Charizard), or press B for one HP; a fully evolved starter can only press B. Eevee branches: its Evolve plaque splits into Vaporeon, Jolteon and Flareon, the table picks one, and that is its last form. About three runs in four evolve at least once and a third reach the final form. Then eight routes of four cards from each route's fifteen (wild Pokémon, trainers, hazards such as Zubat caves and Snorlax, catches (a drink for a ball, and HP back), items, a Pokémon Center (+2 HP), a Poké Mart, and a crossroads whose loud path deals one of the route's two Ace Trainers), each closed by a gym: gym k is one of nine leaders, every region's kth gym from Kanto to Paldea (Alola's Trial Captains and Kahunas stand in), so the lineup is random but still ramps up. After the fourth gym the run detours through one of nine villain hideouts (Rocket, Magma, Aqua, Galactic, Plasma, Flare, Skull, Macro Cosmos, Star: two grunts, then a boss beaten twice), then Victory Road, the rival (beaten twice), a Legendary (1 of 30, two hits), four of 36 Elite Four members (Galar's Champion Cup finalists stand in) and a Champion (1 of 13, three hits, the whole table fights). Items: Potion (down a fresh drink for one HP), Protect (blocks the next loss), X Attack (roll a lost battle again), Escape Rope (escape a wild or trainer battle, never a gym). Statuses: Poisoned (each loss costs one more), Confused (the bag is sealed), until the next gym falls. Zero HP whites the team out and everyone finishes their drink; beating the Champion is the Hall of Fame. Badges fill a row in the team dialog. **Types** (owner, 2026-10-25): the starter has one type for the run (Eevee's changes when it branches), and nearly every fight has the type of the Pokémon on it. A matchup is the rule every player knows: when the starter's type is super effective against the foe's, double the dice are rolled and the best kept (a d20 the better of two, 2d6 the two highest of four); when the foe's is super effective against the starter's, the worse or the lowest are kept; anything else is even. A fight with no dice costs 1 HP less or more when lost. The edge shows as a chip on the strip, green chevrons up or red chevrons down; tapping it explains the matchup. The team dialog lists what the starter is strong and weak against, so the table can plan an Escape Rope, a Potion or a **Tera Orb** (before a battle, the starter Terastallizes into the type that beats the foe, for that fight; Zorua and Eevee start with one, a Tera Raid Den and two Marts sell them). Every route carries three more wild Pokémon and trainers of the types the League was short of, so each starter type meets about as many good matchups as bad. `tests/league.balance.test.ts` plays 4,000 seeded runs: about 23% reach the Hall of Fame (14% with Piplup to 42% with Mudkip; every hideout 20% to 25%), a won run takes about 68 draws (64 to 73), and most whiteouts come at the Elite Four, the Champion and the Legendary. Old saves from the meter-mode League still play to their end. In translating: gyms became leader battles, board movement and lost turns became pours or one-shot dice, and untitled spaces are named after the Pokémon in them. `cabin` (`Cabin weekend`, `src/content/cabin.ts`) translates the CABIIN 2.0 board game in `reference/cabiin-2/`: 85 cards, including the 11 CABIIN-born cards that used to sit in Core and the 8 CABIIN rows of the house sheet (verbatim), all under `cabin.*` IDs; the old `core.*` and `house.sheet-*` IDs are retired. No card is in two packs. Teams became the drawing player or a home-state call-out; board-only spaces, guess-the-roll spaces, spaces that depend on another card, and lore that needs its backstory were left out. `likely` (`Most Likely To`, `src/content/likely.ts`) is 250 standalone vote cards in the spirit of Drunk Stoned or Stupid: on three everyone points, and the most-voted player drinks (2 by default; some prompts pay 3, a shot, or a finished drink). Nothing is tallied between cards. `secrets` (`Secrets & fuses`, `src/content/secrets.ts`) is 156 cards built on the hold-to-read and timer mechanics: 81 secrets (Paranoia, secret missions, Fact or Cap, truth-or-lie stories, secret rules), 63 on a visible clock (16 Forehead, 16 No Saying It, 10 Act It Out, 10 Three in Five and 11 timed challenges) and 12 fuses. A fuse never sends the phone round (owner, 2026-10-06: no broken phones): the phone stays on the table while a beer is passed, and whoever holds the beer at the boom pays. Mechanics are borrowed from Paranoia, Killer, Heads Up, Taboo, charades, 5 Second Rule and hot potato; every prompt is original. Source consequences are kept as written — shots, chugs, gendered splits, mixed drinks and rough dares are not softened. `dungeon` (`Dungeon night`, `src/content/dungeon.ts` and `src/content/dungeon/`, 2026-10-15, rebuilt to League depth on 2026-10-17): the bar has a cellar, and the cellar goes down forever. In the plain modes its 308 monsters, elites, ambushes, trials, traps and bosses play as ordinary cards (a lost fight costs a drink). Its own mode, Pub Crawl, plays the pack alone as a run on 11 shared hearts. **The adventurer** opens it (1 of 18 classes, the League's starters done as a dungeon party): each has one signature you feel (the Barbarian's +2 on boss rolls, the Rogue's traps and bigger satchel, the Bard's natural 20 on a 19, the Warlock's +2 on everything for three hearts) and levels up twice, each level a change the banner names. Since 2026-10-25 every class forks: the first level up (at a landing, or a Dog-Eared Tome, which asks too) picks one of two paths, each a plaque with its name and a short tagline (the Barbarian's Berserker, "Hits bosses and elites", or the Brute, "Big, dumb, hard to kill"), and the second level follows that path. The Drunken Monk starts bad and ends strong (or sobers up). The Bouncer, Bartender, Mercenary and Druid joined then. **Knacks** (2026-10-25): every real class has two of five (Brawn, Sneak, Magic, Charm, Faith), and eight rooms a floor call for one (a trap for Sneak, a ghost for Faith). Facing a room that calls for one of the party's knacks, the strip shows green chevrons with the knack's mark, and the fight is rolled with double the dice, keeping the best (a trial costs a heart less). A knack never hurts: a class without it fights as written. About two fights a run. Three joke classes are the worst by design (owner, 2026-10-25: "intentionally bad", humor first), and both their paths make them worse in their own way, so the table picks its poison or rests: the Plus-One (the Plus-Two and the Plus-Three, or The Ex and The Ex's New Partner), the Intern (Senior Intern and Middle Manager, or Coffee Runner and LinkedIn Guru) and the Mimic, a treasure chest that thinks it's an adventurer (Bigger Chest and Storage Unit, or Self-Aware Chest and Chest With Anxiety). **Tonight's omen** (1 of 20) follows: a boon with a trade-off, such as Flooded Stairs, The Lock-In or Cargo Shorts, or a table rule the app doesn't track. **The queue**: the Quarter-Zips (Brad, Chad and Thad, a rival party doing the same crawl in matching quarter-zips) cut in at the door, one fight for an item (1 of 8). Then three floors, each one of three (Cellar, Wine Cellar or Brewery; Catacombs, Sewers or Walk-in Freezer; Drowned Taproom, Kitchen or Rooftop Bar: 27 dungeons), of eight rooms from the floor's 40 or so, and on the first two a **boss** (one of five; 1 and 2 hits) whose banner is the floor's wanted poster and who **drops a trinket** when it falls. A **landing** follows each boss: level the adventurer up for 3 drinks, or rest for 3 hearts. After the second landing, **the stairs**: the Quarter-Zips are a floor ahead (1 of 5, one fight for an item; 2026-10-25). After the third floor, **closing time** (the Quarter-Zips again, for your table, beaten twice) and **Last Call** (one of eight, 3 hits). Trinkets are boons kept for the run and stacked with the adventurer and the omen (+2 to boss rolls, a heart more, a satchel slot, a Second Wind); elites, behind a fork's loud door, drop them too. A **jinx** is a spoken table rule laid by a lost fight (Goblin Tongue: the jinxed player speaks of themself in the third person), shown on the meter until the floor's boss falls. **The Quaff**: a lost d20 can be rolled again for 4 drinks (3 until 2026-10-17, 6 until 2026-10-25, owner), once until the next boss falls, so drinking buys a second chance. **The Cup**: every lost fight pours a splash into a cup in the middle; whoever lands the last swing hands it to anyone, and on a wipe whoever drew the last card drinks it. Room fights get harder each floor (d20 7+, 9+, 11+; traps 9+, 11+, 13+; bosses 9+, 11+, 12+; rooms cost 1, 2, then 3 hearts). Each floor also holds ambushes (beaten twice), two merchants (one may sell a Dog-Eared Tome, a level up on the spot, for 2 hearts), two forks, treasure, shrines (+2 hearts), relics (walk away or roll) and four push-your-luck gambles (since 2026-10-25; one until then): win to bank a prize, then cash out or go again 2 harder, and a loss forfeits the lot. Mimics (a keg, a coffin, a fridge) pay out until they bite, and the last of three prizes is the best (a Revive, a Tome). The satchel holds three unless the adventurer, omen or a trinket says otherwise: Healing Draught (drink 4, +2 hearts; a fresh full drink for 1 until 2026-10-25, owner), Coaster Shield, Loaded Die, Smoke Bomb, Second Wind and Dog-Eared Tome. A curse (Bleeding, Hexed) lasts until the floor's boss falls. Inspired by Heroes of Barcadia's drink-as-damage fights, Slay the Spire's boss relics and rest sites, Picolo's viruses, Kings Cup's cup and the D&D table "quaff"; every card is original. `tests/dungeon.balance.test.ts` plays 4,000 seeded runs through the real engine with a sensible table: about 25% of runs survive (32.5% before the 2026-10-25 expansion and its three joke classes; real adventurers 21% to 41%, the Plus-One, the Intern and the Mimic 6% to 9%; omens 14% on Open Bar to 35% on The Regulars), a won run takes about 48 draws, and most wipes come at the second boss, closing time and Last Call.

Any combination of packs can be selected; at least one is required. Pub Crawl and Pokémon League play their own pack alone; the other selections are kept for the next game. The 929 cards of the plain modes comprise 92 sip, 377 group, 49 category, 339 challenge, and 72 rule cards, 175 of them dice cards; Pokémon League deals none of them: it plays its own 400 League-only cards (72 gym leaders, 30 Legendaries, 36 Elite Four, 13 Champions, 35 starters, 178 route, Victory Road and rival cards, 36 hideout cards). This distribution is an editorial starting point, not validated balance.

The 2026-10-07 rewrite (owner request: "more creative and crude-funny", all decks) cut Core's bare "roll, then drink or give a number" cards and their duplicates (Core went from 36 dice cards to 16) and added cards where something happens between people. A dice card now states a premise and lets each result carry a punchline with its pour; rules text no longer spells out the outcome table. Across the other packs the same pass reworded math-first dice rules (Pokémon, League, Cabin), punched up flat House rows and the generated VIP cards, and swapped 61 bland Most Likely To prompts for cruder ones. The main deck was trimmed hard on 2026-09-26: near-identical "roll d6, then one of two things" branches, duplicate call-outs and categories, and flavorless filler were deleted. Every remaining dice card resolves to one exact computed instruction (odds/evens, doubles, per-die amounts included) before it is read aloud. All cards are equally likely per shuffle cycle; a deck longer than the pool repeats cards only after exhausting it, and the cycle boundary never repeats immediately when the pool has multiple cards.

The supplied sheet is the authority for the House deck. `src/content/custom.ts` uses its 1-based row numbers in stable IDs (`house.sheet-003` through `house.sheet-105`, excluding blank row 75 and the description-less row 106; `vip.sheet-003` through `vip.sheet-006`). Titles, jokes, threats, and pours are transcribed as written, including the sheet's own typos in titles, with no permission-seeking or softened consequences. The 2026-09-26 logic pass made minimal clarity edits only where the sheet was ambiguous about who acts or how long a lasting rule lasts (`house.sheet-005`, `house.sheet-034`, `house.sheet-082`, `house.sheet-099`, and rows 40 and 53, now `cabin.first-name-only` and `cabin.abra-like-a-slut`); everything else stays verbatim. The eight rows that are CABIIN board spaces (sheet rows 12, 16, 24, 28, 40, 42, 53 and 54) play in Cabin weekend instead, verbatim, as `cabin.maddy-booty`, `cabin.ursaring`, `cabin.wench`, `cabin.no-take-give`, `cabin.first-name-only`, `cabin.pet-that-dog`, `cabin.abra-like-a-slut` and `cabin.whinnie-the-pooh` (owner decision, 2026-10-05). Rows that name dice keep the app roll and compute the pour. Repeated headings such as Categories and Call Out remain separate cards because their prompts differ.

Keep one primary instruction. Aim for 90 characters and 18 words; the build flags longer rules and rejects those over 100 characters or 20 words (long-word titles are rejected over 15 characters). State who acts — the drawing player, everyone, or a named subset — and how long a lasting rule lasts: the rest of the game or until the drawing player's next turn, never until the next card. Specify who starts and when an activity stops where relevant. Preserve stable IDs for unchanged concepts. Text revisions affect new sessions only; active sessions keep their snapshot.

## Review gate

Use the workshop to inspect every card at 320px and enlarged text. Play a Standard (50) game with a real group, then sample more cards across several sessions. Use automated checks for the full 929-card shuffle cycle; a human need not draw 500 cards to review the deck. Record explanations, unclear endings, and repetition before changing the collection. Do not introduce weights or pacing rules without observed need. Lock copy and IDs before any further content work.
